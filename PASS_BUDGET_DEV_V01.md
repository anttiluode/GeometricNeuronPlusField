# Local relinearization pass-budget development v0.1 — more physical passes help, but do not erase the spectral gap

`COORDINATE_CURVATURE_CONFIRM_V01.md` showed that local bond coordinates are about twenty times more curved than direct spectral coordinates at matched task-space trust radius. Because the reciprocal adjoint can be generated by another physical pass through the same medium, the natural engineering question is whether local hardware can compensate simply by taking smaller steps and relinearizing more often.

This development probe used reused bodies 240-243.

All graph schedules used the **same eight local coordinates**, selected once at the base state using the `delta=.01` scale-invariant criterion. The free reference used eight direct spectral coordinates at `delta=.01` for 40 iterations.

Graph schedules were chosen to have the same nominal cumulative task-space trust budget:

```text
G40    delta=.0100 x  40 = .4
G80    delta=.0050 x  80 = .4
G160   delta=.0025 x 160 = .4

F40    delta=.0100 x  40 = .4
```

## Development result

```text
                    mean test C    mean Delta test    graph - free
F40 spectral          0.33246          +0.18396             --
G40 local             0.22197          +0.07347         -0.11049
G80 local             0.26268          +0.11417         -0.06978
G160 local            0.25283          +0.10433         -0.07963
```

Free beats every graph schedule in all 4 development bodies.

## What the extra passes buy

Halving the local trust step and doubling the relinearization count helps substantially:

```text
G80 - G40 = +0.04070 mean held-out C.
```

That is exactly the direction expected from the curvature result: smaller local steps allow the exact physical adjoint to be refreshed before the coordinate response bends too far away from its tangent.

But another halving does **not** continue the improvement:

```text
G160 - G80 = -0.00984.
```

So “just make the local step arbitrarily tiny and take proportionally more passes” is not a complete explanation. With the coordinate set frozen, the local optimizer still follows a different curved path through transfer-function space and can settle into a worse region even with more relinearization.

## Engineering interpretation

The free spectral chart currently has two advantages:

1. it is much straighter under finite moves;
2. a useful large move therefore requires fewer gradient refreshes.

The local physical chart has the implementation advantage that every parameter is one real local coupling and the gradient is available by physical forward/adjoint overlap. Its price is **more physical training passes** and, apparently, a remaining path/coordinate limitation that more passes alone do not remove.

This makes “gradient cost independent of number of tuners” only part of the hardware story. Curvature determines how often that gradient must be remeasured.

## Fresh question

A held-out test should now freeze the development-best local schedule (`delta=.005`, 80 iterations) against:

```text
local .01 x 40
free  .01 x 40
```

and retain `.0025 x160` as a secondary over-relinearized control.

If G80 reliably improves over G40 yet remains behind F40, the hardware trade becomes concrete:

> **local actuation buys physical simplicity but spends extra reciprocal forward/backward passes to cope with a curved coordinate chart.**
